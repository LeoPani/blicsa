DT Article
DE wearable interfaces; multimodal interfaces; sonification; rehabilitation
ID SPATIAL AUDIO; HAPTIC FEEDBACK; MULTIMODAL INTERFACES
AB This study investigates how audio and tactile channels compete for attention when a device narrates the surroundings. We compare sonification schemes against vibrotactile encodings in a within-subject experiment, and assess the approach through reaction time, route deviation and NASA-TLX cognitive load scores. Findings indicate that sparse tactile cues outperform continuous audio once the environment gets noisy. We discuss the implications for  real-time obstacle alerts.
